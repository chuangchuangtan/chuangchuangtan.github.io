  <div class="eyebrow">Research</div>
  <h1>From visual artifacts<br>to trustworthy reasoning.</h1>
  <p>My research asks a simple question: <strong>when visual content can be generated, edited, and manipulated at scale, what evidence can we trust?</strong> I approach this problem from low-level forensic traces, semantic understanding, multimodal reasoning, and agentic verification.</p>

    <div class="eyebrow">Generalizable AIGC Forensics</div>
    <h2>What remains stable when generators keep changing?</h2>
    <p class="chapter-lead">Rather than memorizing generator-specific fingerprints, I study transferable evidence rooted in gradients, frequency behavior, generator architectures, and vision-language representations.</p>

<section class="research-chapter" id="reasoning">
  <div class="chapter-index">02</div>
  <div class="chapter-main">
    <div class="eyebrow">Explainable & Agentic Reasoning</div>
    <h2>Detection should say more than “fake.”</h2>
    <p class="chapter-lead">A trustworthy forensic system should expose what looks abnormal, where the evidence is, and why that evidence supports its conclusion. My recent work therefore shifts from binary detection toward structured visual reasoning.</p>

    <div class="concept-grid">
      <article><span>Evidence</span><h3>Localized visual clues</h3><p>Ground explanations in observable regions and forensic cues.</p></article>
      <article><span>Reasoning</span><h3>Semantic inconsistencies</h3><p>Model object, relation, physical, and commonsense anomalies.</p></article>
      <article><span>Agents</span><h3>Tool-augmented verification</h3><p>Coordinate multimodal models and specialist tools to inspect hypotheses.</p></article>
    </div>

    <div class="project-strip">
      <div><span>ForenDeX</span><p>Explainable forensic reasoning for AI-generated images.</p></div>
      <div><span>AnomReason</span><p>Semantic visual anomaly detection, localization, severity, and reasoning.</p></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-chapter" id="trustworthy">
  <div class="chapter-index">03</div>
  <div class="chapter-main">
    <div class="eyebrow">Trustworthy Multimodal Intelligence</div>
    <h2>Beyond “Is it AI?” toward “Is it believable?”</h2>
    <p class="chapter-lead">The next step is to reason about visual content itself: whether objects, relations, physical properties, and factual claims are mutually consistent and supported by evidence.</p>

    <div class="research-principles">
      <div><strong>Perception</strong><p>Extract observable evidence from images and multimodal signals.</p></div>
      <div><strong>Knowledge</strong><p>Represent physical, semantic, and commonsense constraints.</p></div>
      <div><strong>Verification</strong><p>Actively seek evidence that can support or falsify a hypothesis.</p></div>
      <div><strong>Uncertainty</strong><p>Make confidence and limitations explicit rather than hidden.</p></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
