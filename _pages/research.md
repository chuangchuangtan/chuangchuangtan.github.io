<header class="clean-page-header"><h1>Research</h1><p>My research focuses on trustworthy visual intelligence: understanding what evidence remains reliable when visual content can be generated and manipulated, and how models can explain the evidence behind their decisions.</p></header>

<section class="clean-research-section" id="forensics"><h2>Generalizable AIGC & Deepfake Forensics</h2><p>I study forensic representations that generalize beyond the generators and datasets seen during training. The central question is what traces remain stable as generative models continue to evolve.</p><div class="research-paper-list">

<section class="clean-research-section" id="reasoning"><h2>Explainable Forensics & Multimodal Reasoning</h2><p>Binary labels are often insufficient for trustworthy forensic analysis. I am interested in models that can identify abnormal evidence, localize it, describe it in natural language, and explain why it supports a forensic judgment.</p><div class="research-text-list"><div><h3>AnomReason <span>ICLR 2026</span></h3><p>Semantic visual anomaly detection and reasoning for AI-generated images, moving from a single authenticity label toward structured anomaly descriptions and explanations.</p></div><div><h3>ForenX</h3><p>Exploring multimodal large language models for explainable AI-generated image forensics.</p></div></div></section>

<section class="clean-research-section" id="trustworthy"><h2>Trustworthy Visual Intelligence</h2><p>My broader goal is to connect visual perception with knowledge and reasoning. This includes physical and semantic consistency, uncertainty-aware inference, multimodal evidence, and agentic systems that can use specialist tools to inspect visual hypotheses.</p><p>Across these directions, I am particularly interested in methods that are <strong>generalizable</strong>, <strong>evidence-grounded</strong>, and <strong>interpretable</strong>.</p></section>
